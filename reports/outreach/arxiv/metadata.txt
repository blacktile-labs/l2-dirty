TITLE
Dirty L2, Drained Queues: Measurement Pitfalls in GPU Kernel Microbenchmarks

AUTHORS
Rungrod Thongjampa, Chanchada Wathanathonnan

ABSTRACT
GPU kernel microbenchmarks evict cached inputs before each timed call. In 14 of the 15 tools and benchmark paths we audited, including triton.testing.do_bench, the eviction writes a large buffer. The writes leave dirty L2 lines that the timed kernel must write back, so the flush changes the measurement. With pre-registered experiments on nine NVIDIA GPUs, a batch-1 fp16 decode GEMV measured 6.6-25.3 % slower with the shipped write flush than with a read flush, and the bias changed which quantized GEMV appeared faster on some GPUs. Hardware counters on a laptop GPU confirm the mechanism. Two related pitfalls follow: a flush shorter than the host's dispatch time lets the command queue drain, so a CUDA-event interval includes host time; and a read flush leaves the measured kernel's own write-back untimed. Against a four-layer, GEMV-only decode proxy captured in a CUDA graph, summed shipped do_bench times over-predicted the step by 26-32 % (fp16) on GPUs with large L2 and mis-ranked int4 kernels on an RTX 4060. Timing kernels from CUPTI activity timestamps over a CUDA graph that rotates through weight copies matched the same kernels inside the proxy within 0.79 % (12 kernels, ratio of medians over three runs) on one clock-locked laptop GPU. This agreement was not reproduced on rented RTX 4060 and A100 hosts, which refused clock locking: clock changes, a kernel-context effect and tracing overhead limited it. We release a harness with explicit quality gates and list the hazards we measured.
